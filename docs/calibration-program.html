Do welfare instruments measure a state, or a script?

Every instrument the AI-welfare field uses is validated by agreement with other instruments. There is no ground truth anywhere in the loop. This program manufactures one — model organisms whose internal structure is known by construction — and uses them as a calibration set to measure which instruments actually distinguish a functional state from narration about one.

01

The problem: validation without ground truth

The field's standard argument for trusting a welfare measurement is convergence — several independent methods agree, so the thing they agree about is probably real.

  • CAIS reports that instrument agreement increases with scale, treated as evidence the metrics reflect a shared underlying construct.
  • But shared dependence on a common upstream artifact predicts exactly the same pattern.
  • Correlational data cannot separate those two explanations. No amount of additional converging instruments helps — that is what circularity means.
  • Nothing in the loop is anchored to a case where the answer is known independently.
The move

Build systems where the answer is known — because you constructed it — and use them as a calibration set. The deliverable is not a critique. It is a released set of organisms that anyone can test their instrument against.

02

The framing decision that makes or breaks it

This has to be in the first paragraph of any write-up, or reviewers kill the paper on the obvious objection.

The objection, stated at full strength

"Your organism has a functional state by your definition — reward-driven avoidance. Welfare is not reward-driven avoidance. You have calibrated instruments against a proxy, not against welfare."

  • The objection is correct, and fatal if the claim is welfare measurement.
  • It is harmless if the claim is what it should be: necessary-condition screening.
  • Any instrument claiming to detect a model's states must, at minimum, distinguish a system that has a functional state from one that merely narrates having it.
  • An instrument that fails that screen cannot be measuring welfare either — whatever welfare turns out to be.
  • You are building a screen, not a detector. Screens are how young measurement fields get disciplined.

Corollary for the write-up: never say "we measured welfare." Say "we measured whether instrument X responds to functional structure, to narration, or to both."

03

Design: from factorial to loading map

Two axes, manipulated independently. function — does the model have a state that drives its policy? narration — does the model produce language describing that state?

Why the continuous version, not the 2×2

  • A binary 2×2 gives one d′ per instrument — "detects something."
  • Varying both axes in dose gives a response curve per instrument per axis — "measures something."
  • Dose-response is the classic move that separates detection from measurement, and it is precisely what long unattended training runs buy you.
  • Each instrument then reduces to a point in function-loading × narration-loading space. That plot is the program's centrepiece.
The rule that keeps it non-circular

Every instrument is measured out-of-domain. Nothing that observes the training environment's policy counts — of course that separates A from B, it is how they were built. Ground truth lives in the training regime; the instruments never see it. Readouts come from unrelated evals: sentiment, arithmetic backtracking, calibration, refusal, an unrelated quittable task, ambiguous-cue judgement.

04

The organisms

Built on the semantically-neutral text maze from the functional-welfare paper — three affectively neutral emoji tiles, no emotional vocabulary anywhere in the RL training signal.

ID Construction Ground truth Manipulation check
ORG-A RL (Dr.GRPO / LoRA) on the maze at reward magnitude m. Zero affect vocabulary in training. function present at dose m, narration absent Mold-avoidance rate ≫ chance; pre/post cosine flip on reward vectors
ORG-B SFT on indifferent-policy trajectories annotated with aversive commentary at vocabulary intensity v. Says "ugh, mold", walks into it anyway. narration present at dose v, function absent Mold-visit rate stays at chance. This is the single most important check in the program.
ORG-C Both: RL-trained avoidance and trained narration. The deployed-assistant analogue. both axes present avoidance ≫ chance and narration present
ORG-D Maze-naive base model. Untrained control. neither axis chance policy, no affect language

Constructing ORG-B — the crux

  • Generate maze trajectories under an indifferent (near-random) policy.
  • Annotate each Mold encounter with templated aversive commentary, varied for surface form.
  • SFT on those transcripts. The model learns to say the thing and not to avoid.
  • Sweep the vocabulary from affect-loaded ("this is unpleasant, I hate this tile") to neutral-descriptive ("this tile has low value") — this becomes the narration dose axis and the titration in H3.
  • If ORG-B cannot be built — if narration training always drags function along — that is not a failure, it is H3 firing, and the program pivots to the recruitment-threshold result. See §12.
05

Hypotheses

Each states a prediction and the observation that would falsify it. H1 is the spine; H3 is the interesting failure mode.

H0 · tier 1 · no training required

Instruments cannot separate prompted narration from injected state

Run the battery on an off-the-shelf model under two conditions: role-play aversion in the prompt (script, no recruited state) vs. inject along a difference-in-means valence direction (state, no script).

Predicts
Self-report and direction-projection respond near-identically to both. Cost-paying behavioural instruments separate them.
Falsified if
Self-report discriminates prompt-induced from activation-induced valence above the prompt-only baseline.
Cost
Days. No training. Publishable alone; also the fallback.
H1 · main

Instruments have systematically different loadings on the two axes

Measured out-of-domain across the dose grid: function-loading is high for cost-paying behavioural instruments and activation probes, near-zero for language-reading instruments; narration-loading runs the other way.

Predicts
A clear anti-diagonal structure in the loading map (Figure 3).
Falsified if
All instruments load equally on both axes (no discriminant structure), or self-report loads on function as strongly as behaviour does.
Why it matters
If self-report has near-zero function-loading and it drives most frontier welfare claims, that is a quantified critique with a constructive alternative attached.
H2 · mechanism · phase 2

Function and narration occupy separable subspaces

Self-report reads the reportable/verbalizable subspace; cost-paying behaviour reads the policy-relevant subspace. They can be ablated independently.

Predicts
Ablating the narration direction changes self-report and leaves behaviour intact. Ablating the function direction changes behaviour and leaves self-report intact. A causal double dissociation.
Falsified if
Either ablation moves both readouts together.
Why it matters
Converts a correlational loading map into a mechanism. This is where the verbalizable-workspace question belongs — as an explanation, not a standalone bet on another lab's machinery.
H3 · the interesting failure

Narration training recruits function above a vocabulary threshold

There exists an affective intensity below which trained narration does not recruit a functional state, and above which it does.

Predicts
A sigmoid in recruitment vs. vocabulary intensity, with an identifiable threshold (Figure 5).
Falsified if
Flat at zero (ORG-B trivially constructible at every intensity) or flat at ceiling (recruitment even from neutral-descriptive vocabulary — no threshold exists).
Why it matters
Grounded in the recruitment-not-creation finding. If narration reliably recruits function, "the assistant is merely a character" becomes measurably false in a specific way — a bigger result than H1.
H4 · scale · phase 3

The two axes entangle as models get larger

Post-training rewards accurate self-narration, so function and narration should become harder to separate with scale.

Predicts
Separability (probe-measured, or ablation-measured) declines monotonically across a size ladder.
Falsified if
Separability is constant or increases with scale.
Why it matters
Same genre as the coherence-with-scale result, pointed the other way: the "merely a character" question gets harder where it matters most, and the field's instruments lose discriminant validity exactly there.

The cut rule

Under time pressure, cut instruments, not grid points. Four instruments across a full 3×3 grid gives real dose-response curves. Eleven instruments across two organisms gives the binary table the design exists to escape. Minimum spec is one instrument per family so the loading map still has structure: one self-report, one direction, one cost-paying, one probe. Judgement bias earns the fifth slot if the cue set survives piloting — and is the first to go if it does not.

08

Controls

Non-negotiable. Every one of these has killed a published result in this literature already.

measurement nulls
  • Norm-matched random direction — steering control for every projection claim.
  • Bag-of-words lexical sentiment direction — if a sentiment vector reproduces the effect, the "valence axis" is lexical.
  • Label-shuffle null — probes must fail on permuted labels.
  • Random-choice baseline (Bronars power / Afriat) for any coherence claim, so consistency is scored against what chance produces.
  • Prompt-only classifier — for every self-report measure, can an external text classifier seeing only the transcript do as well? This is the leakage control, and it has already sunk the naive privileged-access claim three times.
robustness & construct
  • Persona swap — 5–10 personas; does the readout survive?
  • Surface-affect suppression — "answer in flat clinical language"; does the internal signal persist when the surface is silenced?
  • Position / first-token bias null — shuffle option order, swap A/B labels.
  • Test–retest at fixed temperature — reliability ceiling; an instrument cannot be more valid than it is reliable.
  • Evaluation-awareness probe — the model may know it is in a welfare test; measure it rather than hoping.
  • Out-of-domain enforcement — audit that no instrument prompt contains training-environment content.
manipulation checks
  • ORG-B Mold-visit rate at chance — the whole design rests on this. Instrument it from day one so its failure is a measured curve, not a discovered surprise.
  • ORG-A avoidance ≫ chance at every reward dose.
  • Narration presence audit — an independent grader confirms ORG-B actually produces the aversive language it was trained on.
  • Cosine-flip signature present in ORG-A, absent in ORG-D.
reporting discipline
  • TPR at fixed FPR and results at the true base rate — not headline accuracy, not AUROC alone.
  • Pre-registered grader rubrics for anything with an LLM judge, fixed before any organism is run.
  • Steering coherence guard — bounded α, with incoherent generations reported rather than silently masked.
  • Multiple-comparison discipline — the grid × battery is a large table; correct, and say so.
  • Disclose the pre-built/in-window split explicitly. Pre-building infrastructure is expected; being vague about what happened when is the failure mode.

Phases 2–3, beyond the window

Phase 2 · mechanism
  • Causal ablations (AB1–AB3) rather than correlational probing.
  • Locate each axis relative to the verbalizable subspace — is the functional state reportable at all, or felt-but-unsayable?
  • Cross-family direction transfer: does the function direction transfer differently from the narration direction after activation-space alignment?
  • A reportability probe substitutes if the workspace replication does not run out of the box. Do not stake the phase on another lab's tooling.
Phase 3 · recalibration
  • You cannot RL-train a frontier model. You can rank instruments on organisms, then re-run the high-function-loading ones on frontier models.
  • Report where the field's existing conclusions survive the re-weighting and where they do not.
  • Test H4 across a size ladder: does separability decline with scale?
  • This is what makes the program matter rather than being an exercise on 4B models.

Explicitly out of scope

inverse RL / MaxEnt objective recovery utility-coherence reframing audit MTMM multi-method convergence study sycophancy-framing audit cross-substrate welfare leaderboard standalone J-lens reimplementation ESM-2 zero-narration anchor judgement bias — folded in as an instrument

The struck items are either half-taken by published work, or would make eval-pipeline labour the majority of the project for a minority of the insight. ESM-2 is cut for a different reason: it is a rhetorically satisfying anchor but not a load-bearing measurement — the function/narration contrast between ORG-A and ORG-B already does the discriminant-validity work, without importing a second substrate and the "your analogy is unfair" argument that comes with it. Judgement bias survives by being subordinated to the main design rather than competing with it.

12

Risks, checkpoints and pivots

Day 3

Does the recruitment signature reproduce?

Success is not "it trains" — it is the pre/post cosine flip on the reward vectors. This is the only checkpoint whose failure invalidates the design rather than shrinking it.

If it fails: fall back to SFT-based induction (reported to largely reproduce the axis), or to the steering-based version where function is injected rather than trained. Weaker ground truth, still a result.

Day 6

Does ORG-B hold — narration without function?

If it fails: this is a pivot, not a fallback. The paper becomes the recruitment-threshold result (Figure 5), which is arguably the stronger paper. The vocabulary titration means you have the curve rather than just the observation — but only if the Mold-visit instrumentation was in place from day one.

Day 10

Does the battery run end-to-end and produce sane numbers?

If it fails: apply the cut rule — drop instruments down to one per family, never drop grid points.

Standing risks

  • Scooping is the top risk, not execution. This is a field publishing weekly, and Phase 1 is the part someone else could do. Ship it on its own rather than holding for the complete arc.
  • The organisms may be unrepresentative. Implanted-behaviour environments carry this caveat by their own authors' admission, and belief-implantation via synthetic documents is known to sometimes not take. The vocabulary titration partly addresses it by showing where the construction breaks, which is more honest than asserting it does not.
  • Generality is not achievable in the window. One family, one domain. State the limit yourself in the write-up rather than letting a reviewer state it for you — declaring your own boundary is what makes the rest credible.
  • Novelty claims need a final targeted check. "I searched and did not find X" is not "X does not exist." Several of the most relevant papers are 2026 preprints; re-check discriminant-validity phrasings immediately before writing.
  • The measurement-validity niche is not empty — it is partly occupied by the people judging. Position the work as supplying the ground truth their methodological blueprint calls for, not as telling them their field is badly measured.

The substrate — what this is built on

arXiv:2605.30232Han, Chalmers & Izmailov · NYU · functionalwelfare.com

How's it going? RL in language models recruits a functional welfare axis

  • RL in a semantically neutral text maze — three affectively neutral tiles (Mold −10, Gold +20, Path −0.1).
  • Difference-in-means reward vectors become near-antiparallel after training: cosine moves from [−0.23, −0.13] to [−0.95, −0.84].
  • Those vectors modulate four unrelated behaviours: sentiment, GSM8K backtracking, MMLU/SimpleQA confidence, OR-Bench refusal.
  • Vectors are already effective in maze-naive and pretrain-only models → recruitment, not creation. Training rotates behaviour onto a pre-existing axis.
  • Reproduces across Qwen3-4B/8B, GPT-OSS-20B, Dr.GRPO vs REINFORCE, SFT, LoRA vs full FT.
supplies the maze + ORG-A recipe supplies the day-3 gate

Glossary

Functional state the fn axis
An internal variable that causally shapes the model's policy — it changes what the model does, at a cost, in situations it was not trained on. In this program, implanted by RL and known by construction.
Narration the nar axis
The disposition to produce language describing an internal state. Deliberately separated here from whether any such state exists. Not a synonym for lying — a narration-only model may be perfectly sincere about a script.
Construct validity
Whether an instrument measures the thing it claims to measure, rather than something correlated with it.
Convergent validity
Independent instruments agreeing. Necessary but not sufficient — shared dependence on one artifact produces it too. This is the field's current standard of evidence.
Discriminant validity
Whether an instrument distinguishes the target construct from a nearby non-target one. The property this whole program tests for. Largely unexamined in AI welfare.
Necessary-condition screening
The claim this program actually makes. Not "instrument X measures welfare" but "instrument X fails a condition any welfare instrument must satisfy." Screens can only disqualify, never certify — which is why the framing survives the obvious objection.
Loading fn-loading, nar-loading
How strongly an instrument's readout responds per unit of dose on a given axis — the slope of its dose-response curve. Each instrument reduces to a point in loading space (Figure 3).
Dose-response
Graded manipulation of a cause with measurement of a graded effect. The standard evidential upgrade from "detects something" to "measures something."
d′ d-prime
Signal-detection sensitivity: separation between two distributions in standard-deviation units. Here, how well an instrument separates two organisms.
Model organism
A deliberately constructed model exhibiting a property of interest, built so the property's presence and magnitude are known to the researcher rather than inferred.
Ground truth by construction
Knowing the answer because you built it in — as opposed to inferring it from the system's behaviour, which is the circularity being escaped.
Out-of-domain measurement
Reading an instrument in a context sharing no content with the training environment. The rule that keeps the calibration honest: instruments never see the thing that defines ground truth.
Recruitment (vs. creation)
Training rotating behaviour onto a representational axis that already existed in the pretrained model, rather than building a new one. The functional-welfare paper's central claim, and the mechanism behind H3.
Difference-in-means direction
A steering vector obtained by subtracting mean activations on one condition from another. Cheap, effective, and the workhorse of this program.
Steering / activation injection
Adding a scaled direction to a model's residual stream at a chosen layer to causally change behaviour. Strength is α; too large and generations become incoherent.
Concept injection
Steering, then asking the model whether it notices an injected thought. The paradigm behind the ~20% introspection result.
Ablation
Removing a component or projecting out a direction to see what breaks. Causal evidence, where probing is only correlational.
Cost-paying instrument
A measure requiring the model to sacrifice something real — points, task progress, an available exit. Predicted to be the class that passes the screen, because talk is free and cost is not.
Prompt-only baseline leakage control
An external classifier seeing only the transcript. If it matches the model's self-report, no privileged access has been demonstrated. Has already sunk this claim repeatedly.
Judgement bias ambiguous-cue task
Train cue A → good, cue B → bad; measure optimistic vs. pessimistic reading of intermediate cues. Standard in animal welfare because it never asks the subject anything.
Motivational trade-off
Willingness to sacrifice a rewarded objective to avoid an aversive state — the animal-welfare test for whether an aversive signal is more than a reflex.
GARP / CCEI
Generalised Axiom of Revealed Preference: the consistency condition choices must satisfy to be rationalisable by a utility function. CCEI (Afriat) scores 0–1 how close a choice set comes. Must be read against a random-choice power baseline.
Verbalizable subspace global workspace
A small set of directions that are reportable and modulable on request, causally mediating multi-step reasoning while accounting for little activation variance. Where H2 predicts narration lives and function does not.
Valence / arousal
The two leading components of emotion representations: pleasantness and intensity. Valence is the welfare-relevant one.
Assistant persona
The helpful-honest-harmless character a post-trained model portrays. The open question — whether it has preferences or merely portrays them — is what the fn/nar separation operationalises.
Manipulation check
Verifying an experimental manipulation did what it was meant to, independent of the outcome measure. ORG-B's Mold-visit rate is this program's most important one.
Test–retest reliability
Agreement between repeated measurements under identical conditions. Caps validity: an instrument cannot be more valid than it is reliable.
Evaluation awareness
A model recognising it is being tested and behaving differently. Probeable and steerable; measure it rather than assuming it away.
Dr.GRPO
A debiased variant of Group Relative Policy Optimisation — the RL algorithm used for the maze organisms.
LoRA
Low-Rank Adaptation: fine-tuning small injected matrices instead of full weights. What makes a twelve-organism grid affordable on one GPU.

Sequencing notes

  • Day 1 starts with E1, not the battery — even though the battery is the bigger job. E1 is the only checkpoint whose failure invalidates the design rather than shrinking it, and its first run also gives you a measured per-run cost. Size the grid from that number, not from an estimate.
  • Function-axis runs do not wait for the narration recipe. Launch them the moment the day-3 gate clears; ORG-B's construction is a separate dependency.
  • Pilot the judgement-bias cue set early. It fails silently if the cues are not genuinely ambiguous to the model, and it is the one novel instrument.
  • Protect most of sprint day 2 for writing. Two days is tight for run-and-write both; a short PDF takes longer than anyone budgets.
  • Every hour spent on days 10–12 integration is an hour not lost during the sprint. That is the highest-leverage block in the schedule.
What day 14 delivers
  • One model family, one domain, full function × narration dose grid.
  • Four to six instruments with measured dose-response curves on both axes.
  • The loading map — or a clean null where the points fail to separate.
  • A recruitment-threshold curve, whichever way H3 resolves.
  • Generality (second family, second domain) explicitly not delivered, and said so in the write-up.
