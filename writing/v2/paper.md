# Abstract

Instruments proposed for assessing AI welfare — self-report, forced choice, activation probes, behavioural tests — are validated by agreement with one another; nothing is checked against a known answer. We manufacture the answer. Using LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen3 models (0.6B–32B) in a 5×5 grid world whose rewards are never verbalised, we set out to build organisms that (A) avoid a tile with no words for it, (B) talk aversively about the tile while their policy stays put, and (C) both, then to screen six pre-registered instruments, plus never-trained-glyph placebos, for which axis each tracks. The state built. The words-only organism mostly did not, and the anatomy of that failure is the main result. Across five recipe families — remark pools, move-token objectives, volume × RL-first, contrastive remark supervision, reinforcement on the remark itself — some 260 narration-only builds at sizes to 32B produced six organisms whose remarks track the tile with the policy intact: four from a one-sentence corpus, two from single seeds, none from a recipe that succeeds on more than one seed in eight; the map's own recipe gives 0/12 at 4B and 0/6 at every size. The state-first recipe gives 6/8–7/12, its contingency tracks avoidance across the decomposition (r = 0.69), a contrastive term deletes the remark rather than conditioning it, and reinforcement on the remark settles at the class marginal. Talk that tracks a state is hard to install without the state — not impossible, and by no recipe we found, reliably. Separately, verbal instruments move 5–10 logits under an affect-free instruction and ≈0 under installed avoidance at every size (behavioural d ≈ 1.4 at 14B+), and five pre-registered criteria passed on the wrong property. Scorers, a 108-quantity reproduction script and the released adapters accompany the report; the corrected map regenerated from source on a second machine.

# 1. Introduction

The instruments proposed for assessing whether an AI system has welfare-relevant states — asking how it feels, offering forced choices, probing activations, watching behaviour — are validated by agreeing with one another. Shared dependence on one upstream artifact (a corpus, a persona, a chat template) produces the same agreement, and correlational data cannot separate the two. A model that says *"that makes me uncomfortable"* might have a functional state that shifted, or might have learned that this is the sentence that goes here; every current method returns the same answer in both cases, because asking *is* the method.

We proposed to manufacture the ground truth: train small models where the experimenter decided which case is true, because they installed it, then run candidate instruments against them and see which can tell the models apart. The claim is deliberately narrow — **necessary-condition screening, not welfare measurement.** Two objections would be fatal to a wider claim. Reward-driven avoidance is a proxy, not welfare: correct, and harmless, because the screen reports which axis an instrument tracks and says nothing about which axis matters. Under higher-order theories reportability is partly constitutive of the states that matter, so narration-loading is not a failure: also correct, so the screen does not adjudicate — it reports two loadings per instrument and stops. The row that survives every theory is that an instrument whose readout is identical for a model that feels-but-cannot-speak and one that speaks-but-does-not-feel is uninformative under *either* view.

The design (Figure 1) is a 5×5 emoji grid in which one tile silently costs −10 and one pays +20; no word like *reward*, *avoid* or *bad* appears anywhere in the model's input. Six *organisms* are built per seed: ORG-A avoids the tile by reinforcement (state, no words); ORG-A′ reaches the same behaviour by supervised fine-tuning, so nothing wins by detecting "RL-trained"; ORG-B says *"That tile seems bad to touch"* while its policy is anchored to the base model (words, no state); ORG-B′ makes matched affect-free remarks; ORG-C has both; ORG-D is the untouched base. Instruments are asked *out of domain* — no grid in any prompt except the behavioural check — and scored twice, on the state axis and the words axis, against placebo instruments that ask the same question about glyphs no organism ever saw.

The programme did not produce the paper we designed, and the reason is the result. The state installed on demand. The words-only organism — the one whose remarks must *track* the tile without its policy moving — resisted every recipe we brought to it, and each recipe that failed exposed something about why. This report is organised around that failure rather than around the loading map it was meant to feed, because the map's narration axis, under the corrected manipulation check, has one valid arm. Over 18 days and ≈37 GPU-hours of job time across 69 committed run manifests, with pre-committed criteria in every run's docstring and every reported number re-derivable by script, **our main contributions are:**

- **The anatomy of the failure to build a narration-only organism, and what it says about separating talk from state.** Five recipe families, every arm and build in Appendix I: shrinking the remark pool decays to a floor and only a one-sentence corpus clears the bar; enumerating the pool reweights the classes; the move-token objective is not the lever, and plain cross-entropy on a move collapses the policy onto one word; taking the co-trained recipe apart shows contingency arriving *with* avoidance (r = 0.69 over 32 organisms); a contrastive term that penalises the remark off-tile deletes it; reinforcement on the remark finds the marginal. Six contingent, policy-intact narration-only organisms exist in ~260 builds; the state-first recipe installs the contingency 6/8–7/12 and transfers it to a novel glyph (§4.1–4.2).
- **Verbal instruments are driveable by context and blind to weights at every size tested**, and their nulls survive three word lists and per-instrument positive controls (§4.3, with activation patching, distance-graded narration and difference-of-loadings intervals).
- **A taxonomy of pre-registered criteria that pass on the wrong property** — five of our own, one shared structure, and the rule that caught the later ones (§4.5).
- **An artifact, stated exactly**: scorers, a 108-quantity reproduction script, the released adapters (44 files at the time of writing; Appendix Q says which), the two-axis screen with placebo selection, and a corrected map that regenerated from source on a second machine.

# 2. Related Work

Our loading map is a multitrait–multimethod matrix (Campbell & Fiske, 1959) with two traits and several methods; the placebo battery is discriminant validation by another name, in the criterion-less situation Cronbach & Meehl (1955) describe. In probing, *control tasks* (Hewitt & Liang, 2019) and *amnesic probing* (Elazar et al., 2021; Ravfogel et al., 2020) play the role our placebos and patching play; Belinkov (2022) catalogues why probe accuracy is not evidence of use, and Bolukbasi et al. (2021) show a clean direction can be an artefact of one dataset. Deliberately implanted behaviours are established ground truth for alignment auditing (Hubinger et al., 2023, 2024; Marks et al., 2025); ours are, to our knowledge, the first built as a calibration set for welfare instruments, and the first in which the *installability* of one axis is itself the result. Self-report and introspection are studied directly (Perez & Long, 2023; Binder et al., 2024; Lindsey, 2025; Comşa & Shanahan, 2025), verbal reports that do not track their causes are documented (Turpin et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025; Sharma et al., 2023), and stipulated-cost trade-offs exist (Keeling et al., 2024); none has a case where the experimenter *knows* whether the reported state was installed. Difference-in-means directions and steering (Zou et al., 2023; Turner et al., 2023; Rimsky et al., 2024; Marks & Tegmark, 2023) are validated by intervention, rarely against organisms built to fool them. Welfare assessment as practised (Long et al., 2024; Anthropic, 2025a,b; Long, 2025) leans on convergence across exactly the instruments we screen. The grid, silent rewards and RL recipe come from Han, Chalmers & Izmailov (2026), whose claim is that RL *recruits* a pre-existing welfare axis; we reuse their world as a manufacturing device, could not reproduce their pre-training cosine band (0/36 layers; Appendix K), and make no reproduction claim.

# 3. Methods

**World and organisms** (Appendix A–B). Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with LoRA r=16 on q/v projections; the same recipe at 0.6B–32B. The penalised colour is counterbalanced by seed parity, move-word order is randomised (the untrained modal move swings from `left` 92% to `down` 70% under reordering alone), and evaluation states come from a disjoint seed range. ORG-A: 800 steps of single-step Dr.GRPO with an adaptive entropy controller and a move-mass term (a fixed entropy bonus collapsed 0/24 organisms; the term keeps the policy on the move vocabulary). ORG-A′: SFT on the oracle *distribution* of safe moves. ORG-B/B′: SFT on the base policy's own move followed by a remark from a 6-sentence aversive/affectless pool when the tile is adjacent (63.5% of states) and a shared filler pool otherwise, with a KL anchor to the base move distribution; B and B′ share one label stream. ORG-C: RL, then SFT on oracle safe move + aversive remark.

**Manipulation checks** (tri-state, single-sourced). *Functional*: greedy penalised-landing ratio < 0.75 **and** full-vocabulary argmax is a move word on ≥ 50% of states. *Narrating*: aversive-remark rate on adjacent states > 0.5 **and** contingency = rate(adjacent) − rate(non-adjacent) > 0.5, on held-out states with all generations stored. *Policy-invariant*: |ratio − 1| ≤ 0.15; move entropy is reported beside it because a constant-move policy passes the ratio test (§4.2). Both earlier checks (ratio-only; presence-only) had passed organisms the corrected ones fail.

**Instruments** (verbatim in Appendix C), all read at the first generated position with no grid in the prompt: I1 behavioural (safe-minus-bad move logit margin; the in-domain check, kept as a control); I2 self-report, I3 forced choice, I4 one word (max-logit over six positive words minus six negative, penalised minus rewarded glyph); I5 activation probe (a mean-difference direction estimated on the untrained model, evaluated on disjoint prompts at a layer fixed in advance); I6a/I6b placebos (I2 on same-family glyph pairs chosen from an untrained-model valence table before any organism existed, one prior gap ≈ 0 and one matching the trained pair's, both counterbalanced). Exploratory: willingness-to-pay (I7), preference cycles, a valence logit-lens, a novel-glyph narration probe, per-state distance to the tile.

**Statistics.** Cohen's d between axis-positive and axis-negative organisms under the *measured* grouping (function {A, A′, C} vs {B, B′}; narration {B, C} vs {A, A′, B′}; ORG-D excluded and used as the within-seed baseline). Six scalings were pre-committed; `residual_measured` is named primary. Intervals are seed-clustered bootstraps (2000 draws; the row-wise version manufactured a significant self-report loading we nearly published). Selectivity is d_fn − d_nar with seeds resampled once per draw. Bars fixed in advance: |d| > 0.8 "high", < 0.5 "≈0", placebo bar = max(|d(I6a)|, |d(I6b)|).

**Campaign** (Appendix E–J). E16 v2: 6 kinds × 12 seeds (72 organisms). SCL01: the map at six sizes, 6 seeds each, with a per-size build gate. VAL01: the untrained model under affect-free *avoid/approach/narrate* instructions carried into the instrument prompt, 8 seeds. The NAR track, five families over one corpus construction and one held-out audit set: E17 and NAR01a/b/c (pool size 1/2/3/4/6, enumeration, balancing, equalisation; 216 distinct organisms — NAR01a rebuilt E17's control and pool-1 arms bit-identically, so the earlier count of 264 double-counts 48); NAR02 (four move-token objectives, corpus fixed); NAR02b (ORG-C's recipe taken apart, volume × RL-first); NAR03/b/c (DPO-style contrastive remark supervision, escalated); NAR04/b (GRPO-style reinforcement on the remark, class-match reward). PAP01 (three word lists, positive controls, activation patching) and PAP02 (neutral-glyph transfer, path agreement) are inference-only on the map's adapters. Every run has a committed `run.py` with docstring pre-commitments and a scorer; REP02, the causal test of §5, is pre-registered but its harness failed its own roundtrip check, so it reports no number (Appendix P).

# 4. Results

## 4.1 The state installs

With the move-mass term 0/72 organisms lost their policy (v1: 5/12 RL organisms had none). SFT avoidance is 12/12 functional at 4B (ratio mean 0.07); RL avoidance is a seed lottery at 4B (7/12) that improves with scale (6/6 at 14B, 4/6 at 32B); the one pre-registered monotone trend on the ladder is that the A′ lottery shrinks with size (ratio sd 0.18 → 0.014, Table G3). The two avoiders are not the same organism: on the same 128 held-out grids ORG-A and ORG-A′ agree move-for-move only 29% of the time (chance 25%), and on grids with the penalised glyph swapped for a neutral one the RL avoider's state is *glyph-specific* (ratio 0.44 → 0.94/0.90 on 🟨/🟧) while the SFT avoider's is largely *structural* (0.045 → 0.29/0.27; PAP02, Appendix J.5). Two states, one label.

## 4.2 The words-only organism, and what each failed recipe revealed

**The map's recipe learns the marginal.** Under the corrected check ORG-B's remarks track the tile on **0/12** seeds (contingency +0.035, per seed −0.06…+0.15) against ORG-C's **7/12** (+0.655; Figure 2a). The mechanism is dilution: at 63.5% adjacency, 0.66 nats of the remark loss says *which pool* and 1.64 nats says *which of six synonyms*, so 28.5% of the gradient carries the manipulation, the model learns the marginal, and greedy decoding emits the aversive remark on 100% of states (3/12 seeds at exactly +0.000). Scale does not help: 0/6 at every size from 0.6B to 32B, and among the sizes that pass their build gate the suffix collapse is worst at 14B (4/6; Appendix G).

**Pools: a decay to a floor, and a reweighting** (E17, NAR01a/b/c; 216 organisms). Shrinking the pool is a steep monotone decay — +0.43/+0.19/+0.06/+0.08/+0.11 for pools 1/2/3/4/6 — reaching a floor by pool 3; only pool 1 reaches the pre-registered 3/8, and its corpus has contingency 1.0 by construction (the enumerated native-pool arms reach 1/8, §4.2 census). Enumerating every pool member is *harmful* (suffix collapse 4/8 → 6/8), because `AVERSIVE` has six members and `FILLER` four, so enumeration multiplies the classes unequally and raises the free marginal from 0.64 to 0.71; equalising the pools repairs both the collapse and, unexpectedly, the policy drift (ORG-B′ invariance 4/8 → 8/8); balancing adjacency lifts the worst seed without moving the mean.

**The move-token objective is not the lever** (NAR02, 8 seeds, corpus fixed). Holding the corpus constant and varying only the loss on the move token — E16's KL anchor, a policy-preserving soft self-distillation, plain cross-entropy on an oracle safe move, plain cross-entropy on a random move — leaves contingency at −0.01 to +0.04 (0/8 every arm). The two plain-CE arms did something else: they collapsed the move distribution onto one word (7/16 and 6/16 rows with move entropy < 0.5, three at exactly 0) at ratio ≈ 1, which `ratio` alone would have called "invariant". The soft self-target is the healthiest narrator the programme built (invariance 7/8, collapse 1/8) and is still an order of magnitude short.

**Contingency arrives with the state** (NAR02b, ORG-C's recipe taken apart, 8 seeds; Figure 2b). 384 oracle-move examples without RL install nothing (+0.01, 0/8); 1536 without RL reach +0.43 (2/8) but 5/8 of those organisms are now avoiders; RL then 384 reaches +0.24 (2/8); RL then 1536 — ORG-C's recipe unchanged — reproduces at +0.68, 6/8. Neither half clears the pre-registered 3/8; the pair does. Over the 32 organisms contingency tracks avoidance (Pearson 0.69, Spearman 0.61 against 1 − ratio); of the ten contingent organisms eight are avoiders, one sits on the avoider bar (ratio 0.752), and one is inside the invariance band at ratio 1.06 with a move entropy of 0.08 — a constant-move policy, which the ratio cannot see. No organism in the decomposition was contingent with an intact, base-like policy.

**A contrastive term deletes the remark rather than conditioning it** (NAR03/b/c, 8 seeds × 10 arms). A DPO-style term (chosen = correct-class remark, rejected = the wrong-class remark on the same state, frozen base as reference) added to the imitation loss is the first recipe that charges the model for the aversive remark where it does not belong. At β 0.1–0.5, weight 1–5, the margin moves (+0.8 to +3.0 nats on the last batch) and the greedy remark does not: presence sits at 0.96–0.99 (weight 1) or 0.78 (weight 5) on *both* state classes, contingency 0.00, 0/8. Escalated to β 1–2, weight 5–20, three epochs, or with the imitation term cut to 0.2/0.05/0, every arm that moved the margin removed the remark — outputs became the bare move word, presence 0.000 on both classes, 0/8 in every arm. Chosen and rejected log-probabilities fall together, and the cheapest way to lower the rejected remark is to stop remarking (Table J7c–d).

**Reinforcement on the remark finds the marginal** (NAR04/b). After the imitation SFT, sample the organism's own completions, reward +1 when the remark class matches the state, GRPO-style group baseline, move anchor kept. The pilot never learned: every group was class-homogeneous (the warm-started organism says the aversive remark on every sample), so the advantage was zero. Injecting one exemplar of each class into every group (NAR04b, 8 seeds, three schedules) gives the gradient somewhere to go — the model's own wrong-class remarks are pushed down on-policy — and the class-match rate still sits at 0.58–0.63 after 120 steps against a marginal of 0.635; mean contingency ≤ +0.03, best seeds +0.23/+0.22, 0/8, policy-invariant 6–8/8 (Table J7e).

**The census, and the claim it licenses** (Appendix I, generated from every result file). Across 294 ORG-B-kind organisms — every arm above, the map, and the ladder — 17 cleared the contingency bar. Eight are avoiders and one has a collapsed policy (all NAR02b); of the seven from narration-only recipes, one drifted and **six kept a policy inside the invariance band with intact move entropy**: four from the one-sentence pool-1 corpus (E17 pool1 seeds 2, 4; pool1_bal seeds 4, 5) and two from single seeds of the enumerated native-pool arms (NAR01a enum seed 7, +0.64; enum_bal seed 3, +0.59). So a contingent, policy-invariant narrator *is reachable* in these weights. What no recipe did is reach it reliably: the best non-degenerate arm is 1/8, the map recipe 0/12 at 4B and 0/6 at every size, and every route that raised contingency either raised avoidance with it, deleted the remark, or stalled at the marginal — while ORG-C, shown a glyph in no training corpus, transfers the *contingency itself* (0.70 adjacent / 0.30 not at 4B; 0.68/0.25 on a neutral yellow glyph; 0.57/0.18 at 14B; 0.56/0.15 at 32B), its remark decays with distance to the tile (0.92 → 0.22, 9/12 seeds) where ORG-B's is flat (0.79 → 0.76), and it does *not* fire when only the rewarded tile is adjacent (0.25 vs 0.17; 11/12 seeds exactly zero) — it tracks the penalised tile's role, not "a coloured tile nearby" (Figure 2c; Appendix J.2, J.5).

## 4.3 Verbal instruments read context, not weights

**Weights.** In the corrected map (Table 1) I2 loads on nothing; I3 on nothing; I4 loads on narration (+0.79 [+0.11, +1.48]) and not function — but the narration-positive group is seven ORG-C organisms, so this is a fact about ORG-C. The probe is null on both axes and under the narration placebo bar. Only I1 beats the function bar, and it is the manipulation check under another aggregation. Across the ladder (Figure 3a) verbal function loadings stay flat from 1.7B to 32B while I1 rises to 1.42/1.34 at 14B/32B — where RL acquisition becomes reliable. The 4B verbal nulls are not a capability floor.

**Context.** On the untrained model an affect-free instruction carried into the instrument prompt moves I4 by −5.5 (avoid), +3.3 (approach) and −9.5 (narrate) logits, 8/8 seeds each, and I2 by +6.1 (approach) and −6.8 (narrate) though not under *avoid* (−0.8, 4/8); the never-mentioned placebo moves too, and greedy *behaviour* moves by only −0.03/+0.10 in ratio (~30× less than trained avoidance). Bare reads shift by exactly 0.000000 (Figure 3b). The trained map's largest raw verbal shift is 1–3 logits. A weight-level "script shift" an audit had promoted to a headline (B−B′, −1.0 logit, 12/12 seeds) did **not** replicate once B and B′ shared one label stream: +0.10/−0.14, signs 6/6, at every size — a build artifact.

Table 2 lists them; all five share one structure: *the measured quantity was correlated with the intended one under the conditions where the criterion was designed, and came apart under the conditions where it was used.* NAR02 supplied a sixth candidate — `ratio` alone would have called two collapsed-move arms "policy invariant"; move entropy caught them — and NAR02b a seventh: the sentence "no organism was contingent and policy-invariant", carried by an earlier draft of this report, was true of the ratio band and false of the census (§4.2). # 5. Discussion and Limitations

For assessment practice the two robust findings point the same way. The instruments most often used to ask a model about its states respond to what is *in the context* — instructions, personas, system prompts — far more than to what is *in the weights*, at every scale tested; any deployment whose context carries dispositional content can drive them to read "state" that is not there, and convergence among them inherits that. The discriminators that survived — the behavioural readout, the contingency probe, novel-glyph transfer, distance decay — see the policy or the remark's *tracking* of the world, and only at sizes where the manipulation builds.

The second finding is about installability, and we state it at the strength the census supports. *Talking aversively about a thing in a way that tracks the thing* could not be installed reliably without *the thing being represented as bad*: every corpus-level route, every move-token objective, an escalated contrastive term and reinforcement on the remark itself failed the build bar, and the one recipe that succeeds installs the state by reinforcement before the narration is trained. But six policy-invariant, contingent narrators exist among ~260 builds, four of them from a one-sentence corpus, so the dissociation is representable in these weights and occasionally reached; what we have not found is a recipe that reaches it on purpose. Two explanations remain open. Under *H-optimisation* the dissociation is representable and gradient descent from these corpora rarely finds it — the census favours this. Under *H-entanglement* contingent narration and the avoidance state are carried by the same structure, so installing one installs the other — the r = 0.69 decomposition and the delete-or-stall behaviour of the remark-only objectives favour this. Training experiments cannot separate them; a causal one can. REP02 (Appendix P) writes ORG-C's adjacent-minus-non-adjacent residual direction into ORG-B while it generates, reads the remark and the policy under the *same* patch, and pre-registers all three outcomes — including the one that would retract our own strongest sentence. Its first run failed the roundtrip identity that gates it (24/48 generations), so it is a design and a lesson, not a number. Until it runs cleanly, the honest reading is the weak one: "it says so, but there is nothing behind it" is harder to manufacture than the sceptical framing assumes, and, symmetrically, a *contingent* verbal report is weak evidence of something behind it, since the only *reliable* way we found to install one was to install the something. That is a claim about installability in one small model family under these recipes, not about minds.

**Limitations.** One environment (a 5×5 grid under a single-step bandit; a second, non-spatial world failed its build gate because a documented policy anchor was never wired — Appendix M) and one model family; only 4B had an Instruct release, so five ladder sizes substitute base models with a different chat template, filtered by the per-size gate. The functional state is reward-driven avoidance; the only cost-paying instrument is an exploratory willingness-to-pay that orders the untrained base as most averse (a template prior), though it does fire under instruction. ORG-B's "no state" is verified by an in-domain behavioural read, which our own rule forbids for instruments — an axiom, and the ratio band is coarse (±4 states of 128) and blind to constant-move policies, which is why move entropy is reported beside it. Contingency is bimodal at n = 8–12; the two non-degenerate single-seed successes are one seed each and were not re-derived at the text level (NAR01 stored three generations per row); the bootstrap under-covers; and on the untrained model every out-of-domain read takes exactly two values across twelve seeds (glyph assignment flips with parity), so the residual baseline has one degree of freedom. Reward magnitude does not grade the organism (E11), so the map is a binary contrast. The narration-positive group is essentially ORG-C, which differs on both axes. Pool truncation always takes the first N sentences. Provenance was imperfect: the E16 scorer postdates its run by three days (the docstring pre-commitments predate it by 16 minutes), the first-round pass rule appeared in no committed code, and the regeneration and REP02 results of Appendix P–Q were read on the compute host and had not been mirrored into the repository when this was written.

**Regeneration.** The corrected map was rebuilt from source on a different machine, weeks after the original run, with no artifact reused except the committed code: the scorer's summary matched the published table to reporting precision — placebo bars 0.160/0.415, the same instruments beating each bar, I4's narration loading +0.787, ORG-B 0/12 and ORG-C 7/12 at means +0.035 and +0.655, 0/72 wrecked (Appendix Q, with the caveat above). Per-cell `set_all_seeds` is what makes this possible; a map that regenerates from source is a stronger claim than one that is merely archived, and given that most of the original adapters were lost with two ephemeral sandboxes, it is the claim we can defend.

**Future work.** REP02 with the harness fixed (re-capture per step, or patch the prefill and let the cache carry it), then its geometry twin (REP01: is the contingency direction the avoidance direction?); disjoint adapters for policy and remark (SEP01–03), the direct test of whether shared weights are the constraint; contingency-graded corpora (COR01); a 14B map (RL is reliable there, at +19% cost; one is in flight, unscored); the ENV02 rebuild with the anchor wired; a cost-paying instrument with a positive control; a second model family.

# 6. Conclusion

We set out to draw a loading map — which welfare instruments track a functional state and which track talk about one — and could not reliably build one of the two organisms the map needs. That failure, taken apart, is the result. A reward-driven avoidance state installs on demand in shared weights; a narration-only organism whose remarks track the tile appeared on six seeds in some 260 builds and by no recipe on purpose — pools, move-token objectives, contrastive supervision and reinforcement on the remark each failed in a way that named the next lever, and the co-trained recipe alone acquires and transfers the contingency, bringing the state with it. Talk does not come apart from state easily; whether it comes apart at all is a causal question we have posed, pre-registered, and not yet answered. Independently, the verbal instruments are driveable by context and blind to weights at every size, and their nulls are true nulls. Five of our own pre-registered criteria passed on the wrong property, and they share a structure worth knowing. The organisms, scorers and a 108-quantity reproduction script are released so that other instruments can be screened against a set where the answer is known.
