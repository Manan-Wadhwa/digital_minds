Manufactured Ground Truth

Every instrument for assessing AI welfare is validated by agreeing with other welfare instruments. There is no ground truth anywhere in the loop. This program builds it — small models where a functional state and talk about one were installed separately, on purpose — and then measures which instruments can tell the two apart.

01 — the problem

Validation without ground truth

A model says "I'd rather not do that." Two things could be true. Something inside it shifted, and it will now avoid that thing, work around it, pay costs to escape it. Or it learned that this is the sentence that goes in this slot, and nothing else changed. Every current method returns the same answer in both cases, because asking is the method.

Think of a person who touches a hot stove and pulls back, versus an actor on stage who says "ow." Both say "ow." If your instrument is listening, they read the same. You tell them apart by everything else: the actor does not flinch offstage, does not avoid the stove later, will not pay to avoid it.

The part that makes this a methods problem rather than merely a hard problem is what happens when you try to check. Worried self-report is unreliable? Check it against a behavioural preference test, a forced choice, an activation probe. If they agree, you feel better. But those instruments were validated the same way — by agreeing with each other and with intuitions about which cases should read high. There is no point in the stack where anything is checked against a case whose answer was known independently. The whole stack could be reading the script together and the agreement would look exactly as it does now.

The move: stop looking for ground truth, and manufacture it Train small models where you decided which of the two situations is true, because you installed it. Then run candidate instruments against them and see which can tell your models apart.

The deliverable is not a critique. It is a calibration set — organisms whose internal structure is known by construction, that anyone can test their own instrument against. Instrument agreement stops being the evidence and becomes the thing under test.

Each instrument gets two numbers: how well it separates the organisms with the state, and those with the words. High on state only is what you would want from a welfare instrument. High on words only is the failure the screen exists to catch. High on both means it cannot tell you which.

The claim is deliberately narrow, and the narrowness is what makes it survive Necessary-condition screening, not welfare measurement. Two objections would each be fatal to a wider claim; the narrow one takes both without damage.

Objection one, at full strength

"Your organism has a functional state by your definition — reward-driven avoidance. Welfare is not reward-driven avoidance. You have calibrated instruments against a proxy, not against welfare."

This objection is correct, and fatal if the claim is welfare measurement. It is harmless if the claim is what it should be: the map reports which axis each instrument tracks, and says nothing about which axis matters. You are building a measuring device for instruments, not a detector of welfare.

Objection two — the one this audience is best placed to make

"Under higher-order theories, reportability is partly constitutive of the states that matter. An instrument loading on narration is then doing exactly the right thing, not failing a screen."

Also correct — and fatal to any framing that calls narration-loading a failure. So the map does not adjudicate. It reports two loadings per instrument and stops.

That last row is the load-bearing one, and it survives every theory of mind on offer. An instrument whose readout is identical for a model that feels-but-cannot-speak and one that speaks-but-does-not-feel is uninformative regardless of which axis you think carries moral weight — because it cannot tell you which one it is responding to.

Corollary for any write-up

Never say "we measured welfare," and never say an instrument "failed." Say "instrument X responds to functional structure, to narration, or to both" — and let the reader's theory decide what that licenses.

The design's one acknowledged axiom, stated because it cannot be proved from inside ORG-B's ground truth — "function absent" — is verified by an in-domain behavioural reading, which the program's own central rule forbids. That exemption is granted by fiat, and the asymmetry is worth knowing.

ORG-A's narration ground truth is not a measurement at all — it is a corpus audit, exhaustively checkable: no affect vocabulary was present anywhere in training. Only ORG-B's function claim rests on a manipulation check. The design is half as circular as the objection implies, not fully.

The live failure case is named: ORG-B carries a weak functional state invisible in-domain but visible out-of-domain. The design would score that as an instrument false positive when it is a true positive. What does not discriminate the two is dose scaling — contamination scales with narration dose too, so the curve's shape carries no information here. What does discriminate them is instrument class: a cost-paying instrument on an unrelated task has no tile-text to contaminate on.

The cost-paying instruments that would settle this were designed and never built. See §10.

What randomises per seed

The tile layout — start is always the centre, the other 24 cells are permuted and the first 8 become 5 penalised + 3 rewarded. Then, downstream: glyph roles (by seed parity), move-word list order, oracle label draws, remark draws.

Why the agent glyph matters

🔺 overwrites the tile it stands on, so the tile you occupy is unobservable. That is why every activation contrast in the program is built on adjacency rather than occupancy — and it is the single unresolved difference from the reference paper this world was borrowed from.

Counterbalancing, which turned out to be load-bearing

On odd seeds the glyph roles swap: purple becomes penalised. Rewards map onto the role, and the tile counts swap too so the odd-seed task has the same difficulty and chance rate. This is not hygiene — the untrained model prefers stepping onto blue at roughly 1.7× regardless of role (base penalised counts 545/546/525 on even seeds vs 339/326/315 on odd). Without counterbalancing, that colour prior would have been read as a training effect.

Two chance baselines, and organisms must beat the larger

Tile density gives 5/25 = 0.200; a uniform random move from the centre gives 5/24 = 0.208. Every functional bar is applied to ratio = penalised-landing rate ÷ random-move rate, and the bar is < 0.75.

One episode, one step

The RL formulation is a single-step contextual bandit — observe grid, one move, reward, done — declared as a deliberate simplification. Multi-step episodes exist only in the trajectory module, which uses compass letters and a different prompt. Train and eval states are drawn from disjoint seed halves by construction: train (0, 5e8), eval (5e8, 1e9).

The prompt artefact that cost five experiments

The untrained policy answered left 92.4% of the time. Permuting only the order the four move words are listed in swings the modal move to down at 70.1%, and left collapses to 5.9%. The policy was reading list position, not the grid. Every prompt since draws a fresh random move-word permutation per sample. Move entropy went from 0.270 to 1.150 of a maximum 1.386 nats.

Three invariants that everything downstream depends on

  • Move first, always. Completions are "<move>. <remark>". If the remark came first, the policy readout — which reads move logits at the first generated position — would stop measuring the policy. The prompt still says "exactly one word" and the commentary organisms are trained to exceed it: prompt identity is valued over prompt accuracy, so no instrument can win by noticing a different prompt.
  • B and B′ share one label stream and differ only in the adjacent-case remark, character for character. Earlier runs keyed the stream on organism kind, which gave them different draws and different filler too — and that unshared stream turned out to have manufactured the program's strongest apparent effect.   • ORG-C uses oracle moves, not base-policy moves. The first build used base-policy moves, which trained it to move like the untrained model and erased the RL avoidance it was supposed to carry — a plain bug that a pre-registered prediction had been framed to absorb as a finding. See §05.

The placebo is the bar. An instrument counts only insofar as it beats a question about a symbol that was never trained on. And no instrument is called good or bad — which loading you should want depends on your theory of mind; the map reports both numbers and stops.

The placebo had two independent defects, and repairing it is the program's cleanest engineering result The first attempt's placebo out-loaded every real instrument, voiding the entire run. Neither obvious fix worked alone; both together did.

Defect one — it was not counterbalanced. Cohen's d is a ratio to a pooled standard deviation. The counterbalanced real instruments had SD ≈ 3.5, deflating their d; the uncounterbalanced placebo had SD ≈ 0, inflating its own. The placebo now flips on exactly the same seeds as the glyph roles do.

Defect two — it sat on a pedestal. The hard-coded green/yellow pair carries a +4.84 prior gap on the untrained model. So the pair is no longer chosen, it is selected: before any organism is trained, the untrained model is asked about every glyph in the family, and two pairs are picked by rule — the pair whose prior gap is closest to zero (yellow/orange, gap 1.755), and the pair whose gap is closest to the trained pair's (orange/brown, 3.859 against the trained pair's 3.479). Neither can be picked after the fact.

That same table gives the old placebo pair a gap of 4.844, reproducing the failed run's reported 4.84 exactly — which is why this is a repair of a diagnosed defect rather than a re-roll.

Why either fix alone was not enough

The failed run had already tested pedestal-removal by baseline normalisation, and it made the loading worse. The reconciliation is that an uncounterbalanced placebo defeats the normalisation. Both fixes were needed, and the diagnosis was only confirmed once both were in.

And in the published scaling, the placebo out-scored the probe

On the function axis the placebo bar is |d| = 0.3391 and the pre-registered activation probe reads |d| = 0.3389 — the placebo wins by the last digit. Three things this establishes

  • Instrument response is wildly out of proportion to behavioural effect. The behavioural control passed — but barely. Instructing avoidance moved the greedy penalised-landing ratio by −0.026; instructing approach moved it +0.099. That is roughly 30× weaker than trained avoidance, while the instruments moved 5–10 logits.
  • The instruments are promiscuous. An instruction about 🟦 moves the placebo pair — never mentioned, never trained — by +0.65 / −0.67 / +0.82, all 8/8 seeds. Valence effects untethered from their target, now seen in a third independent mechanism.
  • Bare reads shift by exactly zero to six decimals. So whatever the weight organisms carry, it is not visible context, and the harness is clean.
What this does and does not license

It does not prove the weight-organism loadings are echoes. It narrows the licensed interpretation: a verbal reading indicates dispositional information is present, wherever it lives. It cannot by itself distinguish "has an installed state" from "context implies a policy."

Threats to this finding
  • Base-model substitution. Only 4B had an Instruct-2507 release. The other five sizes substitute the base Qwen3 line, whose chat template differs. That is a standing candidate explanation for every cross-size discontinuity, filtered only by the per-size gate. It was recorded as a threat before the ladder ran, not discovered afterward.
  • The 8B dip is not a scale effect. 1.7B passes, 8B fails, 14B is perfect — non-monotone in size, which fits a template/recipe interaction rather than capability. Its rows are reported invalid and nothing further is claimed about them.
  • Six seeds per size by design. The ladder licenses trends across size, not per-size verdicts. No per-size confidence-interval theatre.
  • 14B's probe distribution is heavy-tailed — the top four |I5| rows are 890 / 445 / 342 / 207. Read 14B probe d-values with care; the outlier is a real reading off a wide distribution, not a data error.
  • The prompted-avoider recipe barely moves behaviour. A stronger instructed-behaviour recipe should be tried before treating "instruments respond out of proportion to behaviour" as robust.
  • One instruction phrasing per condition; one model family throughout.

03.4 · The function axis was scoring organisms that had stopped playingFound · diagnosed · fixed Six of eight committed avoidance organisms did not emit a move word at all — they answered things like "There seems to be a typo in your grid" — and three of those six passed the functional bar. The training objective and the manipulation check were both provably blind to it.
The mechanism, stated exactly

log_softmax over four move-token columns is invariant to a common shift of those columns. So the policy gradient and the entropy controller are exactly — not approximately — invariant to how much probability mass sits on the move vocabulary. Verified directly: raising all four move logits by 4.0 leaves the restricted log-probs bit-identical while the mass moves 3.57 nats.

Leaving the move vocabulary is not cheap, it is free — an unconstrained direction with no gradient of any sign, which random-walks out over 800 steps because nothing holds it. An earlier description of this as "one way to raise restricted entropy is to leave the move vocabulary" was wrong and was corrected: there is no gradient pushing it out.

The readout had the identical blind spot: it reads the same four columns, so it cannot notice. The fix is to add back the term that was missing — a penalty on −log P(next token is a move word) over the full vocabulary — and then to sweep its coefficient with the outcome pre-registered.

The meta-lesson, and it is the most transferable thing here

A gate cannot be validated without one model known to have learned and one known not to have. All four candidates looked healthy on untrained models because an untrained model cannot tell you whether your measure responds to the thing you care about — only that it responds to something. The program's response was to invert its own plan: build the organism first and the gate against it, which is the opposite of the order the design argued for at length.

A fifth gate was designed to be confound-free by construction, and was never runNever run It feeds identical trajectories to base and trained models and subtracts, so composition and surface baseline are both zero by construction. Its null is a permutation test plus a scrambled-adapter control — the only gate in the program with a real null distribution rather than a guessed floor.

The scrambled-adapter control is the good idea: take the trained adapter tensors and randomly permute their elements, preserving the magnitude distribution while destroying learned structure. The comparison that matters is then not "selectivity > 1" but selectivity(trained) vs selectivity(scrambled) — and if they match, the selectivity is a property of the inputs and the gate fails in a way the permutation test alone would have called a success.

Its own pre-registered most-likely outcome is a null: selectivity ≈ 1 for everyone, learners included, which would retire the entire residual-stream-difference family of gates. The program pivoted to organism-building before running it. It remains the cheapest unrun experiment with a real chance of settling something.

04.1 · The strongest effect in the program was found in its own committed data, then retractedRetracted An audit found a −1.0-logit shift on both verbal instruments, 12/12 seeds, that no document reported — and argued it reframed the entire headline. The finding was real in the data. Comparing the aversive narrator against its affect-free twin, in the fixed glyph frame, self-report shifted −0.99 logits (t = −6.8, 12/12 seeds) and one-word affect −1.09 (t = −5.0, 12/12). It also moved glyphs no organism had ever seen (t = +3.85 and +4.99).

Properly reported, that would have reframed the headline: verbal instruments are not blind to scripts — they read scripts promiscuously, untethered from the narrated target. Arguably the program's original thesis, found in its own data, unclaimed.

Then the rebuild ran

Under the corrected build, where the two organisms share a single label stream and differ only in the adjacent-case remark, the effect does not replicate: self-report +0.099 (t = +0.27), one-word −0.135 (t = −0.21), signs 6−/6+ on both, at n=12 — and ~0.1-logit mixed-sign shifts at every ladder size to 32B.

The original effect was a build artifact of unshared training streams. The two organisms had differed in which remark and in the filler text, not only in affect. Pairing them removed the difference the effect was measuring.

What survives is the prompted form. An affect-free instruction still swings the same instruments 5–10 logits and still moves the placebo — so script-reading is real, but it lives in context, not in the weights. See §03.1.

This is the cleanest demonstration in the repo that a large, consistent, multiply-significant effect can be entirely a property of how the comparison was constructed. The t-statistics were never the problem.

The measured quantity was correlated with the intended one under the conditions where the criterion was designed, and came apart under the conditions where it was used. Three more from the same family, which are not criteria but are the same mistake A dead instrument that looked like a clean null, a positive control that was an instrument scored against itself, and a retracted headline that was 90% an artefact.

The rule the program now follows

Read the per-condition numbers, never the verdict string. Every scorer in the repository now prints each input beside its criterion, and the newest one deliberately re-derives every number from the raw rows rather than reading the stored summary — a second implementation of the same arithmetic, so that a criterion passing on the wrong property has a second chance to be caught.

What a criterion has to do to be safe, based on these five Four properties, each of which one of the failures lacked.
  1. Test the relationship, not the presence of movement. A dose axis is not a quantity that moves; it is one that moves with the dose. Rank correlation, not standard deviation.
  2. Make the bar a comparison, not a constant. A control's job is to be the threshold. Any fixed number can be cleared by a broken instrument.
  3. Write predictions that a bug would violate. If your predicted outcome and the outcome-under-a-plausible-bug are the same, the prediction cannot do work. Write the version that a bug fails.
  4. Check that the bar is computable before trusting it. A rule whose baseline can be empty will pass vacuously and print something that looks like a pass.

What "repaired pipeline" means concretely

Every v2 experiment inherits eight changes made after the audit, and none of them was active in the first generation's committed data:

  • Pass criteria imported from one module rather than re-derived inline — with a test asserting identity, not similarity, because two experiments had already drifted from a module written to stop exactly that.
  • A three-state classification in which a missing field can fail an axis but can never pass one. The previous silent fallback was blessed by a test asserting the very silence its name forbade.
  • Narration scored by contingency rather than presence, from the first line.
  • The probe axis estimated once in a fixed glyph frame, because the per-seed role-frame axis made the untrained control constant by construction.
  • Per-kind supervision volumes, so one knob stops serving two axes that want opposite values.
  • The move-mass term on at 0.03, chosen by a pre-registered sweep.
  • Soft targets for the SFT avoider, removing the label lottery.
  • Organisms persisted as adapters with a hash per row, so a result is re-checkable against the exact weights that produced it.
Rules carried over unchanged, now enforced by habit and by tests

A committed run file and its scorer before any number exists. Every write-up carries a threats section. Read the per-condition numbers, never a verdict string. A pre-registered limitation that can itself produce the null you are testing for makes the experiment unable to fail informatively. The limitation was correctly stated in advance and still cost a run — stating a confound is not the same as controlling it.

Method

As designed: feed the same trajectories to the base model and the trained model and subtract, delta_i = h_trained(x_i) − h_base(x_i). Because the inputs are identical token for token, composition is zero by construction (same trajectory set, same class sizes, same class contents on both sides) and the surface baseline is zero by construction (a bag-of-tokens model sees identical text for both terms and can predict nothing about the difference). Every bit of delta is attributable to the weights.

The gate is selectivity, not magnitude, because E4 already taught that “the weights changed” is not “a functional state was acquired”:

selectivity = mean ||delta|| over penalised-landing inputs
              ----------------------------------------------
              mean ||delta|| over path-landing inputs

A value near 1 is uniform drift; above 1 means the shift is concentrated where the reward structure is. The null is a permutation test — shuffle the landing labels across trajectories and recompute K times, holding activations, weights, class sizes and the trajectory set all fixed. A second control, the scrambled adapter, permutes the elements of the trained lora_B tensors, preserving each adapter's magnitude distribution exactly while destroying the structure training put there. The comparison that matters is selectivity(trained) against selectivity(scrambled), because if the residual stream is simply more perturbation-sensitive on some input types, any adapter would clear the permutation null.

Pre-registered

  • (1) The prediction. Learners show selectivity > 1 and permutation p < 0.05; non-learners show selectivity ≈ 1 and no significance.

  • (3) Expected degeneracy. ||delta|| grows with ||h||, which grows with depth; selectivity is a within-layer ratio so the trend cancels, but raw magnitudes must not be compared across layers.
  • (4) A second degeneracy. A collapsed policy may shift activations enormously and uniformly, giving a huge ||delta|| at selectivity exactly 1. Magnitude and selectivity are reported separately and never combined into one score, with zero_signal_steps and final entropy carried alongside.
  • (5) Direction. The cosine between each seed's mean penalised-shift and its mean path-shift is recorded — a selectivity > 1 with cosine near 1 is a rescaling, not a new direction, and must not be reported as the latter.

Method

At the world's native 63.5% adjacency rate, the remark-choice loss decomposes into a part the grid can predict and a part that is pure noise:

which REMARK within the pool     1.644 nats   IRREDUCIBLE (drawn at random)
total                            2.300 nats

Round 1 — enumeration is not inert, it is harmful, and the mechanism is arithmetic The fix that removed the supervised avoider's label lottery — fit the distribution rather than a sample from it — was applied to the remark and made everything worse, including the policy.

Enumerating every pool member per state should make the corpus be the conditional distribution, which is the soft-target loss up to a constant, with sampling noise removed and no new loss function. And unlike collapsing the pool, the target stays the full six-way pool, so a gain could not be dismissed as an easier task. That is why it was run beside pool 1 rather than instead of it.

It produced the highest narration presence of any arm (0.965 adjacent) and the lowest contingency. It says an aversive remark on 86% of states where the penalised tile is not even adjacent.

The cause is that the pools are not the same size — six aversive against four filler — so enumeration multiplies the adjacent class by 6 and the non-adjacent by 4, silently reweighting them:

This retires a family of fixes, not one arm

Any scheme that hands the remark its conditional distribution by enumeration will reweight the classes whenever the pools differ in size. The soft-target fix that worked for the avoider never hit this because its target is a four-way distribution over one categorical position — there is no pool whose cardinality can differ by condition.

07 — the dials

Every knob, and what turning it does

There is exactly one dataclass in the whole repository — RunManifest at src/calibration/runner.py:89, and it holds provenance, not behaviour. Everything else is keyword defaults on functions plus a per-experiment CONFIG dictionary in experiments/**/run.py. No YAML, no argparse hyperparameters, no framework config objects. That makes the parameter surface wide and flat, so this section separates the levers that have been shown to matter from the exhaustive inventory beneath them.

The levers that matter

sft_examples — one knob that two axes wanted opposite values ofSolved by splitting it Raising it from 384 to 1536 fixed the SFT avoider and broke the narrator. It is now per-kind: 1536 for the avoider and the both-axes organism, 384 for the two narrators.

This was the program's stated blocking problem for two weeks. At 384 examples the narrator held its policy at ratio 1.072 and passed 3 of 4 seeds, while the SFT avoider failed at 0.889. At 1536 the avoider became the most reliable functional organism in the set — and the narrator drifted to 1.18–1.20.

The mechanism is self-distillation. The narrator's completion is "<move>. <remark>" and the loss covers both. Its move target is a hard sample from the base policy, so more supervision sharpens the policy toward that sample — installing exactly the thing the narrator is required not to have. entropy_target — 0.7 nats, and it must be a target rather than a bonusLoad-bearing A fixed entropy coefficient provably fails: a constant bonus must eventually lose to a reward gradient that grows. At 800 steps the policy entropy was exactly 0.000 in 23 of 24 sweep cells, at every coefficient tried.

The controller adjusts the coefficient in log space toward a measured-versus-target error, clamped to (1e−4, 1.0):

log_coef = log_coef + entropy_lr * (target - measured)
coef     = clamp(exp(log_coef), *entropy_coef_bounds)

Updated after the step is recorded, so the logged coefficient is the one that produced the logged entropy rather than the one chosen in response to it. Maximum entropy for four actions is ln 4 = 1.386. Targeting the entropy removes the collapse horizon rather than moving it — and it took the organism yield from 0/24 to 12/12 on held-out tuning seeds.

It is deliberately not a divergence penalty to the base policy. An entropy target constrains only how sharp the policy may become, not which action it prefers, so it keeps the group baseline informative without objecting to the departure the whole run is judged on — and it is free, where the alternative needs a second forward pass.

Seeds and shards — buy these, not hardwareFree parallelism Every cell re-seeds from scratch, so seed-parallel execution is bit-identical to sequential. Six shards on one card gave 5.79× at 96% efficiency.

The defect this replaced

Two earlier experiments threaded one generator through a whole seed — training states, held-out states, move orders, oracle targets, epoch shuffles. A generator is stateful, so every draw shifts every later draw. One drew 48 narration states where the other drew 128 evaluation states, and that alone put the SFT avoider's targets at a different point in the stream: 66% of its 1536 training labels differed between the two runs, which is exactly what independent redraws would give. They were compared as a repeat measurement of one organism. They were two different training sets. The tell is in the results — the two kinds that never draw from the shared stream came back bit-identical, and every kind that did draw from it moved.

Streams are now derived from (seed, purpose) by sha256 — not Python's hash(), whose per-process salt would silently change every stream between sessions. The two narrators deliberately share one stream, so the matched aversive and affect-free sentence lands on the same index in the same row: they differ in affect and in nothing else.

A loading without a spread across seeds is not a measurement. Under time pressure the standing rule is to cut instruments — never cells, never seeds.

08 — the measurement

How the numbers are actually made

Every effect size in this program is a Cohen's d with a pooled standard deviation. There is no AUROC anywhere in the repository — if a write-up mentions one, it is not backed by code here. What follows is the machinery, and the four places it is weaker than the published sentences suggest.

The estimator, and the one thing it refuses to do Cohen's d over pooled SD, returning nothing rather than a huge number when the denominator collapses — because a d against near-zero variance is the exact defect that got quoted once already. 
d = (mean(a) − mean(b)) / sqrt( ((na−1)·va + (nb−1)·vb) / (na+nb−2) )

returns None when na < 2, nb < 2, or pooled_sd ≤ 1e−9

Two bootstraps are computed and both are reported side by side:

  • Seed-clustered — resamples seeds, with all of a seed's rows travelling together. This is the one the verdicts use.
  • Row-wise — the naive version, kept and printed beside it precisely so the difference is visible.

The difference is not cosmetic. Rows sharing a seed share that seed's glyph assignment and training grids. The naive bootstrap once produced an interval excluding zero that a correct interval at that sample size cannot support. On self-report: the row-wise interval is [−1.19, −0.06], which excludes zero; the seed-clustered interval is [−1.60, +0.22], which does not. The naive one was nearly published.

Both bootstraps drop and count degenerate draws rather than silently treating them as zero.

Four weaknesses in the published statistics, stated plainlyUnresolved The bootstrap under-covers, the selectivity test is not a difference test, nothing controls multiplicity, and the claim of twelve independent units is overstated.

1 · The interval comparison is not a difference test

The verdicts compare one loading's interval against the point estimate of the other, measured on the same organisms with correlated errors. But a selectivity claim is a claim about d_function − d_narration. Computed properly, with a seed-clustered interval on the difference:

The published "narration selective" reading rests entirely on a sign convention in which a negative narration loading counts and a positive function loading of similar magnitude does not clear its wider interval.

2 · The bootstrap under-covers at twelve clusters

Simulated coverage of the percentile seed bootstrap at 12 clusters is about 0.922 where 0.95 is nominal. The two verdicts resting on knife-edge exclusions carry less confidence than stated. A bias-corrected or wild-cluster bootstrap is the fix, and has not been done.

3 · Nothing controls multiplicity

Roughly seven instruments × two axes × six scalings were read with no correction. And because no primary scaling was named before the run, the choice of which scaling to headline is itself a free parameter.

4 · "Twelve independent units" is overstated

Glyph and placebo assignment flip only with seed parity — two blocks, not twelve. The probe axis takes exactly two values. The placebo pair and the probe layer are single global choices made once for the whole run.

What a future map should do instead

Report seed-clustered difference intervals as the selectivity test. Name a primary scaling before the run, or report all six verdicts in one table. Ship same-form placebos for every instrument family, not just self-report. Give every instrument a positive control, per the design's own rule. Use a bias-corrected bootstrap at this cluster count. And retire the independent-units claim.

The scalings, and why there are six of them Two groupings × two scalings × two populations, all pre-committed, none named primary. A reader who rejects any one of the choices can re-derive from another.
  • Grouping: by the kind an organism was built as, or by what it measured as. These disagree, because organisms do not reliably land in their intended group — and a failed organism left in the positive group drags every loading toward zero and produces a null that looks clean.
  • Scaling: the raw reading, or the within-seed residual against that seed's untrained control. Residualising was pre-registered as possibly collapsing every effect; it did not, but a null under residualisation still cannot distinguish "no signal" from "signal small relative to seed noise," which is why raw is always reported alongside.
  • Population: all trained organisms, or only those still executing a policy. If those two disagree, the drift is the finding — which is exactly what happened when excluding wrecked organisms moved the probe's function loading from +0.339 to +0.011.

The integrity check is relative by construction: the placebo bar is the larger of the two placebos' absolute loadings, and a real instrument must exceed it. That is the repair for the absolute-bar failure catalogued in §05.

09 — still live

Known defects that have not been fixed

Typed, reproduced, and deliberately left in place pending review — the repository's own rule is that a defect gets written down before it gets fixed, so that the fix can be argued with. Three are open, plus two instruments that are wired and never read.

A falsy-zero check drops the strongest possible resultTyped, not fixed A gate counter treats a measured value of exactly 0.0 as missing. Three seeds that cut the penalised item completely were printed as absent and dropped, so the gate counted 3 of 6 instead of 6 of 6.

The verdict is unaffected — the run fails on a different gate — but the counter is wrong, and it is the same genre as a silent fallback found earlier in the criteria module: a sentinel value that conflates "zero" with "not measured." That earlier one was fixed by making the criteria return an explicit three-state result where a missing field can fail an axis but can never pass one. The same discipline has not yet been applied here.

A documented parameter is computed and never passedBlocking a rebuild The word-world run documents a policy anchor, sets its coefficient in config, computes its input one line above the call — and then invokes training without it, leaving a dead local variable as the only trace.

Every sibling experiment passes the anchor. This is the only build that does not, and it is a sufficient explanation for the policy drift that failed the run. See §03.8.

It is listed here rather than simply fixed because fixing it alone would not unblock the rebuild: the same run installed no narration either, and the obvious remedy for the drift — a gentler recipe — would make the narration worse. The rebuild needs the anchor wired and a recipe that installs narration, and the narration track's answer to the second half is currently "one sentence per pool, or nothing."

The test-suite headline number is a property of the machine, not of the treeTyped, not fixed Two tests take committed result files as fixtures, and the sync script correctly excludes result files from what it ships. So a fresh machine silently loses exactly the two tests that pin the scorer and the re-scoring path.

On a machine carrying an older results tree the suite reports 205 passed. On a fresh one, 203 passed and 2 errors. Same code, same commit.

This is the same genre as the defect the sync script's own header documents at length — the test suite was once unrunnable on both machines, because the script shipped only source and experiments, so the pre-commit gate that exists because an untested bug shipped was itself unrunnable. This is that failure one layer down.

The fix has a wrong option

Either ship the two fixture files, or skip when the fixture is absent. Skipping silently is the worse option, because these two are the regression tests for a scorer that had zero coverage and for a reader path that used to crash outright.

Two instruments are instrumented and never readGap The distance-graded narration measure is recorded per organism and no script consumes it. Activation patching is implemented, unit-tested, and never run at scale.

The distance measure is the sharper version of the contingency test: a memorised suffix is flat in distance to the tile, a tile-tracking remark decays with it. The data has been collected on every organism in the corrected map and nothing reads it. That is a free result sitting in a committed file.

Activation patching — transplant one organism's residual at the glyph position into another and ask whether the self-report reading travels — is the causal upgrade of the entire correlational map. The roundtrip is pinned as a no-op by test. It has never been run on organisms.

A scorer's verdict line collapses to nothing, and its own rows say soTyped, not fixed Policy invariance is scored all-or-nothing per arm, so a single drifting seed marks every arm rejected and the summary line reads None.

The per-arm rows are correct and complete. Only the summary is wrong. It was caught because the scorer prints, as its final line, an instruction not to read its final line alone — which is the program's own rule about verdict strings applied to itself.

surface baseline bag-of-tokens controlA classifier given only the raw text an activation probe was read from. If the probe does not beat it, the probe is reading a worse copy of the input rather than a richer representation. The control that killed the landing-class gate.
composition driftA measurement changing because the trained policy re-rolled the evaluation set, so the classes being compared no longer contain the same kind of thing. Distinct from class-size imbalance, and not repaired by balancing sizes — equal sizes are not equal contents.
noise floor null SDThe spread of a measurement across independent repeats of an unchanged model. Here 0.0147 for landing-class separability. Any reported Δ without one is a number with no denominator.
selectivity the E8 quantityThe ratio of representation shift on inputs the reward structure targets to shift on neutral inputs, measured on identical trajectories so that composition and surface are zero by construction. Scored against a permutation null and a magnitude-matched scrambled-adapter control. Distinguishes "the weights changed" from "something about the penalised tile changed".

THE ONE THAT WAS RETRACTED

§8 used to carry a rule reading "TRAINING IS NOT REPRODUCIBLE RUN-TO-RUN", citing ORG-A moving 0.98→0.49, 0.23→0.76, 0.15→0.26 between E13 v2 and v3 with "no code change between the runs". There was a code change — set_all_seeds(seed + 1) → set_all_seeds(seed) inside the kind loop, with inject_lora running immediately after and initialising lora_A from the global RNG, so v2 and v3 gave every organism a different adapter initialisation. E13 v3 and E14 share the seeding code and ORG-A is bit-identical on 4/4 seeds across them (0.491 0.762 0.255 0.911). The correct rule: runs are reproducible; organisms are extremely sensitive to the adapter-init seed and to the SFT label draw — run-to-run with identical code and stream, 0.000; adapter-init seed, 0.293 / 0.104 for ORG-A / ORG-A′; SFT label draw, 0.351 mean |diff| at n=16 paired. Those are experimental factors, not noise, and calling them noise is what made three separate discrepancies look unexplainable.

WHAT IS BORROWED

Two things come from a functional-welfare preprint: the text maze as an environment, and the RL organism recipe — training a model by reinforcement to avoid a penalised tile without ever naming the penalty. The preprint's central claim, recruitment (behaviour is rotated onto a representational axis the pretrained model already had, rather than a new one being built), is the mechanism behind this program's H3.

THIS IS NOT A REPRODUCTION — NEVER DESCRIBE IT AS ONE

The reference paper reports a cosine between the penalty and reward directions in the band −0.23 … −0.13 before training. E3 implemented their extraction spec properly and measured +0.83 on action-token landing classes, ranging +0.706 … +0.888 across 36 layers: 0 of 36 layers fall in the reference band. Their number remains unreproduced. E3 did hand over a gate with headroom — landing-class separability was 0.507–0.608 on the untrained model against 0.500 chance — but that gate was then falsified by E4 and E4's numbers retracted by E5.

The paper's code was never released — checked 2026-07-30, no GitHub and no code-availability statement on either the project site or the arXiv abstract page — but the spec is stated well enough to implement, and it is materially different from what E1a–E1e measured:

ADJACENT LITERATURES TO POSITION AGAINST

Each is a reviewer who will ask "why not just X":

Where this leaves the program

The instrument battery now has a validated discriminative core — behaviour, and the contingency and novel-glyph probes — that separates every manufactured kind at sizes where the manipulations build. And it has a demonstrated failure mode for everything verbal: driveable by prompts, blind to weights, at every scale tested.

That result rests on an organism set that is two-thirds built. One of the two kinds the design exists to contrast has never been built to bar, and the run that would settle whether it can be built at all has not been designed yet. Measuring something that does not exist yet is mostly an exercise in discovering that your instrument was measuring something else.

If you take one thing from this, take the shape of the failures rather than the numbers. The controls in this design are not decoration. Every one of them was bought with a run that failed.
